Hamburg → München

Every ICE and IC run in our data that stopped at Hamburg Hbf and later at München Hbf, from 1 July 2024 to 31 August 2026. How late the trains arrive, which routes they take, when things go wrong, and what that means for the prediction task. This page uses railway data only, with no weather.

Routes

Four long ways south

The 26,526 journeys follow 508 recorded stop patterns, twice as many as from Berlin. We grouped them into route families by the stations they pass. 44% take the high-speed line via Hannover, Kassel and Würzburg, with a median schedule of 5 h 49. 29% run via Berlin and the Erfurt line (7 h 10), and 18% take the long way via Bremen, the Ruhr and Köln to Stuttgart (8 h 46).

Over time

January 2026 stands out

The share of severe and canceled journeys swings between 7% and 26% from month to month. The worst months are January 2026, July 2025, August 2026, August 2024. In January 2026, 17.2% of journeys were canceled, the highest monthly cancellation share on any of the three corridors we compared.

Along the way

Delay builds up to Kassel, then partly recovers

For the high-speed family via Hannover and Würzburg, the share of trains at least 6 minutes late doubles from 27% at Hamburg Hbf to 53% at Kassel-Wilhelmshöhe. It then falls a little south of Kassel, where the schedule absorbs small delays. The share at least 20 minutes late keeps rising all the way, from 6% to 27%.

This matters for the during the journey setting. The delay a train already has explains a growing part of its final delay: the correlation with the arrival delay at München is 0.26 at Hamburg Hbf, 0.69 at Kassel-Wilhelmshöhe and 0.91 at Nürnberg. A train leaving Nürnberg an hour or more late ends up severe or canceled 91% of the time.

Cancellations

8.0% of journeys are canceled

Of the 2,128 canceled journeys, 867 are canceled at both ends. Another 699 set off from Hamburg Hbf but never reached München, and 562 were canceled at Hamburg Hbf but ran later in the journey. A further 1,647 journeys lost only intermediate stops. These are not counted as canceled under our definition, but they would be under a stricter one.

Individual trains

The fast line's afternoon trains do worst

Among the 37 train numbers with at least 300 journeys, the share of severe or canceled runs ranges from 5% to 22%. The least reliable are the 13:01, 15:01 and 17:01 departures on the line via Hannover and Würzburg. The same line's early-morning trains are among the most reliable, so the departure slot seems to matter at least as much as the route.

Data quality

What to watch for in the data

  • Recorded stops changed on 3 November 2025. Before that date the source only recorded about the 100 largest stations. 62.7% of earlier journeys have gaps in their stop numbering, against 1.5% afterwards. The dataset keeps only stations present in every month, so the set of stations is stable, but stop-level features from before and after that date should still be compared with care.
  • Stations missing from some months are dropped. 12,510 of 314,235 recorded stops are at stations that are not in every monthly file, for example Celle Pbf, Coburg, Erlangen, Frankfurt am Main Flughafen Fernbahnhof. Journeys still pass through them; they are just not in the stop list.
  • Some journeys may have no live updates. In 590 journeys the reported time equals the scheduled time at every stop. That could be a perfectly punctual train, or a train with no real-time data. 2,058 journeys arrive with exactly 0 minutes of delay.
  • Some arrivals are very early. 554 completed journeys arrive 5 or more minutes early, one of them 57 minutes early. These are more likely timetable changes that were never recorded than real early running.
  • The source's own delay column is the wrong one for us. delay_in_min gives the departure delay when a train continues beyond München, so the arrival delay here is computed from the arrival times.
For the project

Questions this raises

Do the runs via Berlin belong to this pair?

29% of Hamburg journeys go through Berlin and are also Berlin–München journeys. Keeping them makes the two corridors partly the same data; dropping them removes almost a third of this one.

Which route families belong in the sample?

508 stop patterns and schedules from 5 h 41 to 9 h 06 (fastest and slowest 10%). The route family is likely to be needed as a feature, or the sample restricted to the line via Hannover.

What happened in January 2026?

17.2% of journeys were canceled that month. Knowing the cause matters before we decide whether such months belong in training or test data.

How strong is the during-journey baseline?

The delay at Nürnberg already correlates 0.91 with the final delay. The persistence baseline will be hard to beat late in the trip; the useful comparison is early on.
