Hamburg → Frankfurt

Every ICE and IC run in our data that stopped at Hamburg Hbf and later at Frankfurt (Main) Hbf, from 1 July 2024 to 31 August 2026. How late the trains arrive, which routes they take, when things go wrong, and what that means for the prediction task. This page uses railway data only, with no weather.

Outcomes

Seven in ten within 20 minutes, four in ten punctual

The severity classes follow the working definition in the project instructions. Delay is measured at Frankfurt (Main) Hbf as reported arrival minus scheduled arrival. A journey counts as canceled when its Hamburg Hbf departure or its Frankfurt arrival is canceled.

Routes

One fast line and three slower ones

The 21,557 journeys follow 388 recorded stop patterns. 66% take the high-speed line via Hannover and Kassel, with a median schedule of 3 h 42. 13% leave that line after Kassel for the older route through Gießen (4 h 40), 15% go west via Bremen, the Ruhr and Köln (6 h 03), and 5% via Berlin and Erfurt (6 h 31).

The routes fail in different ways. Via Köln the median arrival delay is 23 min, against 7 min on the high-speed line, and 38% of journeys are moderately late. Via Gießen the median delay is only 2 min, but 19% of journeys are canceled, and the route has almost no runs after December 2025.

Over time

January 2026 was the worst month

The share of severe and canceled journeys swings between 6% and 26% from month to month. The worst months are January 2026, July 2026, July 2025, November 2024; the best are April 2026, December 2025, December 2024. In January 2026, 14.3% of journeys were canceled.

Along the way

Most of the delay builds up before Hannover

On the high-speed line, the share of trains at least 6 minutes late rises from 23% at Hamburg Hbf to 42% at Hannover, in the first hour and a quarter, and then more slowly to 56% at Frankfurt. The share at least 20 minutes late grows from 7% to 25%. 81% of all runs start before Hamburg Hbf, but unlike on the Frankfurt–München corridor this hardly matters: 24% of them leave Hamburg at least 6 minutes late, against 26% of trains that start there.

This matters for the during the journey setting. The delay a train already has explains a growing part of its final delay: the correlation with the arrival delay at Frankfurt is 0.36 at Hamburg Hbf, 0.69 at Hannover and 0.79 at Kassel-Wilhelmshöhe. A train leaving Kassel-Wilhelmshöhe an hour or more late ends up severe or canceled 88% of the time.

Cancellations

7.9% of journeys are canceled

Of the 1,709 canceled journeys, 882 are canceled at both ends. Another 505 set off from Hamburg Hbf but never reached Frankfurt, and 322 were canceled at Hamburg Hbf but ran later in the journey. A further 707 journeys lost only intermediate stops. These are not counted as canceled under our definition, but they would be under a stricter one.

Individual trains

Gießen and Köln trains do worst

Among the 30 train numbers with at least 300 journeys, the share of severe or canceled runs ranges from 3% to 33%. Three of the six least reliable run via Gießen: they are rarely late when they run, but often canceled. Two run via Köln, with median delays over 30 minutes. All six most reliable trains use the high-speed line and leave Hamburg between 03:20 and 10:24.

Data quality

What to watch for in the data

  • Recorded stops changed on 3 November 2025. Before that date the source only recorded about the 100 largest stations. 45.3% of earlier journeys have gaps in their stop numbering, against 1.9% afterwards. The dataset keeps only stations present in every month, so the set of stations is stable, but stop-level features from before and after that date should still be compared with care.
  • Stations missing from some months are dropped. 7,099 of 159,543 recorded stops are at stations that are not in every monthly file, for example Celle Pbf, Marburg (Lahn), Siegburg/Bonn, Frankfurt am Main Flughafen Fernbahnhof. Journeys still pass through them; they are just not in the stop list.
  • Some journeys may have no live updates. In 737 journeys the reported time equals the scheduled time at every stop. That could be a perfectly punctual train, or a train with no real-time data. 1,504 journeys arrive with exactly 0 minutes of delay.
  • Some arrivals are very early. 459 completed journeys arrive 5 or more minutes early, one of them 70 minutes early. These are more likely timetable changes that were never recorded than real early running.
  • The source's own delay column is the wrong one for us. delay_in_min gives the departure delay when a train continues beyond Frankfurt, so the arrival delay here is computed from the arrival times.
  • Only Frankfurt (Main) Hbf counts as the destination. 5,339 further Hamburg Hbf runs reach Frankfurt only at the airport or at Frankfurt (Main) Süd and are not in this corridor. The airport station is also missing from some monthly files, so it is dropped from the stop lists.
For the project

Questions this raises

Which runs are shared with the München corridors?

2,559 runs (12%) continue from Frankfurt to München and are also Hamburg–München and Frankfurt–München journeys. A split into training and test data has to keep each run on one side.

What happens to the Gießen route?

It carries 13% of all journeys, almost all of them before January 2026. A test period in 2026 would contain a different mix of routes than the training period.

Should the destination include the airport and Süd?

Counting them would add about a quarter more runs, most of them at the airport. Whether they belong depends on whether the question is about Frankfurt Hbf or about reaching Frankfurt at all.

How strong is the during-journey baseline?

The delay at Kassel, about 80 minutes before Frankfurt, already correlates 0.79 with the final delay. A model has most room to add value at departure and around Hannover.
