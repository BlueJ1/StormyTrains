Frankfurt → München

Every ICE and IC run in our data that stopped at Frankfurt (Main) Hbf and later at München Hbf, from 1 July 2024 to 31 August 2026. How late the trains arrive, which routes they take, when things go wrong, and what that means for the prediction task. This page uses railway data only, with no weather.

Outcomes

Only four in ten are punctual

The severity classes follow the working definition in the project instructions. Delay is measured at München Hbf as reported arrival minus scheduled arrival. A journey counts as canceled when its Frankfurt (Main) Hbf departure or its München arrival is canceled.

Routes

Two main routes of similar length

The 22,544 journeys follow only 72 recorded stop patterns. 55% run via Würzburg and Nürnberg with a median schedule of 3 h 15, and 43% via Mannheim or Darmstadt, Stuttgart and Augsburg (3 h 50). The remaining 2% are diversions and a few trains that loop through the Ruhr and Kassel.

The two main routes differ by about half an hour and have almost the same share of severe or canceled journeys (13.8% and 13.7%). The route family matters less here than on the Berlin or Hamburg corridors.

Over time

June 2026 was the worst month

The share of severe and canceled journeys swings between 8% and 25% from month to month. The worst months are June 2026, July 2025, October 2025 and January 2026; the best are the two Decembers. In June 2026, 14.7% of journeys were canceled.

Along the way

Half the trains are already late at Frankfurt

87% of these trains start before Frankfurt Hbf. 55% of them leave Frankfurt at least 6 minutes late, against 19% of trains that start there. On the route via Würzburg, the share at least 6 minutes late rises from 52% at Frankfurt to 68% at Aschaffenburg, only half an hour later, and ends at 62% in München.

This matters for the during the journey setting. The delay a train already has explains a growing part of its final delay: the correlation with the arrival delay at München is 0.65 at Frankfurt (Main) Hbf, 0.81 at Würzburg and 0.92 at Ulm. A train leaving Ulm an hour or more late ends up severe or canceled 93% of the time.

Cancellations

7.4% of journeys are canceled

Of the 1,664 canceled journeys, 721 are canceled at both ends. Another 573 set off from Frankfurt (Main) Hbf but never reached München, and 370 were canceled at Frankfurt (Main) Hbf but ran later in the journey. A further 603 journeys lost only intermediate stops. These are not counted as canceled under our definition, but they would be under a stricter one.

Individual trains

Late-morning trains do worst

Among the 30 train numbers with at least 300 journeys, the share of severe or canceled runs ranges from 3% to 27%. The least reliable are the 10:22 and 11:54 departures via Würzburg and several afternoon trains via Stuttgart. Early-morning departures via Würzburg are among the most reliable.

Data quality

What to watch for in the data

  • Recorded stops changed on 3 November 2025. Before that date the source only recorded about the 100 largest stations. 17.9% of earlier journeys have gaps in their stop numbering, against 0.2% afterwards. The dataset keeps only stations present in every month, so the set of stations is stable, but stop-level features from before and after that date should still be compared with care.
  • Stations missing from some months are dropped. 4,296 of 144,661 recorded stops are at stations that are not in every monthly file, for example Frankfurt am Main Flughafen Fernbahnhof, Günzburg, Montabaur, Siegburg/Bonn. Journeys still pass through them; they are just not in the stop list.
  • Some journeys may have no live updates. In 323 journeys the reported time equals the scheduled time at every stop. That could be a perfectly punctual train, or a train with no real-time data. 1,567 journeys arrive with exactly 0 minutes of delay.
  • Some arrivals are very early. 297 completed journeys arrive 5 or more minutes early, one of them 57 minutes early. These are more likely timetable changes that were never recorded than real early running.
  • The source's own delay column is the wrong one for us. delay_in_min gives the departure delay when a train continues beyond München, so the arrival delay here is computed from the arrival times.
  • Frankfurt Airport is not in the stable station list. Its long-distance station is missing from some monthly files, so it is dropped from the stop lists. Trains that stop at the airport but not at Frankfurt Hbf are not in this corridor at all, and neither are the 219 journeys that pass only Frankfurt (Main) Süd.
For the project

Questions this raises

What does the model know at departure?

87% of trains are already running when they reach Frankfurt, and half arrive late. Whether the delay at earlier stops is known at the prediction time is part of the kickoff decision on the prediction cutoff.

Is the short corridor a useful contrast?

At 3 h 22 it is the shortest of the three, with only 72 stop patterns and far fewer gaps in the stop data before November 2025 (18%).

Where does the test period fall?

June 2026 was the worst month and lies near the end of the data. A test period in summer 2026 would be harder than the training period.

How do we handle an imbalanced target?

Only 13.8% of journeys are severe or canceled. Precision–recall based metrics and class-balanced baselines fit better than accuracy.
